01 — About

I didn't leave healthcare. I extended it.

Dr. Enosh A PaulsonBDS · Healthcare AI & Data Science

My BDS gave me a clinical understanding of patients, diagnosis, workflows and the day-to-day realities of care delivery. That is the part most AI work is missing.

Over the course of my dental training I became increasingly interested in technology, data and artificial intelligence — and in what actually happens when a model meets a clinic. That took me toward medical informatics, healthcare analytics, machine learning, deep learning and digital health, and into a postgraduate programme in AI & Data Science in Healthcare at IIHMR Bangalore.

I am an emerging healthcare AI professional, not a senior research scientist — and I would rather be precise about that. What I do have is a clinical vantage point, a fast-growing technical toolkit, and projects built end to end on real healthcare problems: imaging models that quantify their own uncertainty, hospital forecasting, segmentation, product design for clinicians, and research on how hospitals can govern AI responsibly.

I care most about the unglamorous half of healthcare AI — calibration, uncertainty, governance, and whether a clinician can actually trust the number on the screen.

Clinical grounding

Bachelor of Dental Surgery and a full clinical internship. Patients, diagnosis, radiographs, treatment planning — and a first-hand view of where clinical decisions actually get made, and how much of the day is spent on things software could carry.

Phase 02

Healthcare data

Medical informatics, healthcare analytics, health information systems and digital health. Hospital admission forecasting across six facilities, EMR ecosystems, interoperability, and the reporting layer that turns messy operational data into something a manager can act on.

Phase 03 · now

Artificial intelligence

Machine learning, deep learning and medical imaging AI — with uncertainty quantification, explainability and the governance that has to come with them. Building models that flag what they don't know instead of guessing confidently.
